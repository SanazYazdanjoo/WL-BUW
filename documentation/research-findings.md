# Early tutor survey

**Survey date:** September 2026

**Responses at the time of this note:** 4

**Participant group:** Welcome Lounge tutors only

**Evidence status:** Preliminary and directional; this small group is not representative of International Office staff, students, or future tutors.

## Repeated questions

Participants generally reported encountering repeated questions.

## Information fragmentation

A strong recurring theme was that information is spread across different places and students may not know where to look. WhatsApp messages can be missed, and students may be unsure whether information applies to them.

## Internal continuity

Participants reported occasional loss, duplication, or unclear transfer of information between colleagues.

## Existing tools

Respondents generally rated familiar tools such as Excel, WhatsApp, and shared documents as relatively easy to use. The opportunity is to add structure and connect existing workflows, not to replace every tool.

## WhatsApp

WhatsApp remains valued as a communication and community channel. The app should hold stable, structured information; WhatsApp should remain available for conversation, unusual cases, and human support.

## Maintenance

Responses about semester maintenance were mixed. This suggests maintenance knowledge is unevenly distributed, so semester preparation should be predictable and transferable.

## Design implications

- Centralize navigation to essential information and preserve canonical official sources.
- Keep WhatsApp for human support and community.
- Keep staff workflows lightweight, attributed, and easy to hand over.
- Retain Excel import/export compatibility.
- Make semester maintenance predictable and documented.

These are design implications from preliminary tutor feedback, not measured outcomes or claims about all users.
